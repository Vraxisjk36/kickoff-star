## Locked foundations

- One world date; leagues keep country-specific start dates, breaks and registration windows.
- One stable ID per player, club, association, sponsor, contract and event. Names can change without breaking saves.
- The club pays from its own currency, cash and annual squad-cost headroom. Proposed wages also respect its current wage hierarchy. Live offers, transfers and loans use `src/engine/proClubFinance.ts`.
- Direct pro starts use a three-week trial, four low/mid-level offers and an explicit playing-time/loan risk statement.
- Gazette articles and social posts are views of recorded world events. Sponsors require a persistent profile and contracts.
- Country tax estimates must be dated and labelled as game estimates; never use one country's tax or eligibility rule worldwide.

## Phase 1 audit checkpoint

`npm run audit:pro-world` checks 75 associations, 1,228 generated clubs, 29,472 active players and 19,092 current-season fixtures; a 20-year rollover, migration, saves, budget-aware transfers and resumed simulation. The generator uses original fictional identities and provisional calendars and finances. Its scope demonstrates scale, not researched parity with real leagues.

## Phase 2 and 3 audit checkpoints

`npm run audit:pro-phase2` runs the 21-day assessment, four affordable offers, matchdays, saves and a 35-year roster simulation. Retirements now usually lead to independent market signings; academy prospects graduate when their potential and a squad need fit.

`npm run audit:pro-phase3` checks the retirement of an 88-rated veteran, a distinct successor and shirt number, position-sensitive aging, injury history, dated contract talks and counters, monthly pay, a loan and return, an in-window transfer, expiry renewal and voluntary retirement. The pro screen displays contracts, housing, medical history and money entries. The ledger uses fictional withholding coefficients, not tax law. Cross-currency transfer fees currently use nominal game units without exchange conversion. Registration windows are provisional and work permits are not modeled. Phase 4 and release QA must resolve these gaps. Pro fixtures are automatically resolved, and device playtesting is outstanding. No AAB or public release should be made from this checkpoint.

## Phases 1–3 polish checkpoint

Direct-pro signing now requires completed talks; the signed youth handoff is an explicit first-contract exception. Monthly wages and housing are prorated for a partial first or final month, and expiry pays the final period before renewal talks. Recovery blocks training in the UI and engine. Save loading supplies fields missing from earlier pro prototypes. Academy succession requires a matching position and never automatically inherits the retired player's shirt. Build, youth season audit, 20-year world audit, 35-year lifecycle audit and Phase 3 flow audit pass.

Remaining release gates: device playtesting and performance profiling, interactive pro fixtures, authored league and registration rules, cross-border currency accounting and eligibility, and fuller club finance reconciliation. The generated world is still a development model, not the final 75-league content edition.

## Phase 4 generated competition checkpoint

`npm run audit:pro-phase4` checks 75 domestic cups, twelve continental tiers, six national association tournaments, four-year world national and 32-club championships, distinct seeded entrants, confederation membership, same-day fixture conflicts, minimum club rest, champions, player call-ups, save resume determinism and save size. European tiers use a 36-club, eight-opponent league stage before a sixteen-club knockout. The African top tier uses a sixteen-club group stage, the South American top tier uses thirty-two, and the Asian top tier uses twenty-four. Four-team continental groups play home and away; national groups use a shorter round robin. These are **original game competitions** with provisional qualification allocations and simplified knockout and calendar rules; they do not claim official governing-body equivalence. All 75 full leagues feed the club tournaments. Thirty-six extra CAF associations join the eighteen African full leagues for complete 54-association national-tournament representation; they have no domestic clubs or continental club qualification yet. A player with a senior pro contract can earn a national cap by reaching their association's eligible top 23; caps and goals are tracked separately from club appearances and bonuses. The world clock now resolves a date's fixtures when advancing *past* the date, preventing matches being marked played before the user's matchday. Preliminary qualifying rounds, other confederations' satellite associations, domestic promotion/relegation, detailed nation-specific cups, and full senior international calendars are not authored. The 35 “playable” league flag remains a target: pro fixtures still auto-resolve and are not yet interactive. The football-world panel surfaces the player's league, cup, relevant continental competitions and national tournament, with regional and world browsing. The 20-year simulation completes with saves below 32 MB, but low-end phone profiling is pending.

## Phase 5 media and commercial checkpoint

The pro career now has a saved audience counter, shared match/club-event news feed, sixty fictional sponsor profiles across six categories, timed offers for two to five years, eligibility by appearances/reputation/audience, acceptance and expiry, and monthly endorsement payments conditional on appearances. Each profile displays a fictional history, four featured athletes and three partner clubs. Signature boots require 120 appearances, 85 reputation, 100,000 followers and an active boot sponsor; the player names the product once. `npm run audit:pro-phase5` checks deterministic offers, terms, expiry, payouts, audience and the rare boot gate. These are generated commercial prototypes; athlete/club associations are illustrative fictional profile entries, not endorsements connected to the actual world roster. Gazette/social entries are currently concise event summaries, with longer authored reporting and richer persistent world feeds still needed.

## Phase 6 competition integration checkpoint

This pass expands the generated football world to 218 associations: 75 full domestic leagues and 143 background national sides. UEFA has 55 associations, CAF 54, CONCACAF 41, AFC 47, CONMEBOL 10 and OFC 11 in this fictional-world registry. Background associations can enter confederation tournaments, European and North American Nations Leagues and world qualifiers, but have no domestic clubs or player call-up path. The registry is not an official federation or FIFA eligibility database; some associations may have different real-world competition statuses.

Europe now has a third, 36-club Conference Series with six distinct league-phase opponents. Entrants across the three club tiers are disjoint. The European Nations League has 55 sides in four seeded divisions, while the North American Nations League has 41 in three. Both have a top-division knockout champion and promotion/relegation between editions. Their fixtures and playoffs are simplified game formats. Six confederation world-qualifier tournaments run in the year before the four-year, 48-nation World Nations Championship; their champion and ranked table influence the next draw. Twelve groups send their top two plus eight third-placed teams to the round of 32. Initial 2026 entry remains seeded because a prior edition is not present in a new save.

Domestic additions include five top-flight-only League Cups (England, Scotland, Portugal, Japan and South Africa) and thirteen two-club Super Cups (England, France, Spain, Italy, Germany, Portugal, Netherlands, Belgium, Türkiye, South Africa, Brazil, Argentina and Japan). These are fictional simplified versions: the England League Cup does not yet contain lower divisions; the Spain super cup is not its real multi-club format. France has a main cup and super cup, with no extra league cup in this edition. All cup schedules use collision/rest checks. `npm run audit:pro-phase6` checks tier separation, background representation boundaries, divisions, champions, 2029 qualifiers feeding 2030, cup score consistency, resume determinism and the 32 MB save ceiling. A 20-year world audit also passes. Continental national championships now follow an edition cadence: Europe, South America and Oceania in 2028 and every four years thereafter; Africa in 2027 and 2028, then every four years; North America in odd years from 2027; Asia in 2027 and every four years. The field is simplified and currently includes every modeled association instead of a separate confederation qualification campaign.

Remaining Phase 6 release gates: interactive pro matches, authored association-by-association rules and statuses, true qualification paths and international windows, all relevant lower divisions, broader international event calendar, audited country finances and eligibility, full translated UI, mobile performance profiling and hands-on Android QA. The 35 planned playable league flags still share automated match resolution. No public expansion AAB is ready from this checkpoint.
